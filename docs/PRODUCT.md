# Phase 1

The visitor is a nonhuman explorer discovering human artifacts. Scope remains prologue, Apollo 11 and a Mars signal teaser only. Mystery precedes naming; the emotional arc is curiosity, discovery and recognition.

The opening keeps one invitation over the Moon and a visible fictional spacecraft. Landing uses a close forward-facing third-person view, a larger ship, an approaching ring, authored hills and a low dust plume near touchdown. Two compact instruments show telemetry and the target map. Flight assist is off by default, including retry. WASD/arrows or touch steer; release stops drift, and held Space/brake softens touchdown. Assistance is explicitly optional.

After landing, walk along authored footprints or skip the walk. Scan the unknown structure to identify Apollo 11. Miso offers one optional random question. Wrong answers receive a gentle, clear correction and the right answer.

Six discoveries reveal brief alien interpretations: Eagle, footprints, camera, seismometer, retroreflector, plaque/disc. Eagle starts complete, assembled and unselected, explicitly labeled as a historical reconstruction. See what remains today switches to its lower stage. Selecting a part pulls it outward and outlines only its edges in blue. Nine simple learning lines, a photo, a Miso quote and an equation float alongside it. Cross-section and full separation remain available. Clicking a selected part again clears its highlight, checkmark, isolation and poster; learning progress is retained. Three parts still gate Eagle's archive action. Footprints are immediately visible and ready to archive without exposure adjustment. Other clues retain brief schematic interactions. Six records unlock the evidence conclusion and Mars teaser.

FIELD GUIDE opens after identification on a white reading surface: mission goals, crew roles, an expandable six-stop timeline, science results, and one page of short English explanations for ages 10-15, expandable real NASA photographs and an interactive Apollo source-code explorer. It covers lander design, lunar photography and three software jobs. It is optional, not another chapter or a new progression gate. Source links and accurate photo captions accompany the content.

NASA facts and historical evidence are distinguished from fictional alien interpretations and schematic reconstruction. Occasional sky streaks and aurora-like glows are decorative fiction, disabled for reduced motion. No exact archaeological survey claim. All assets are local; no new generated film or public deployment was made for this revision.

A separate visible Apollo code entrance opens a repository preview and an interactive code lesson. The preview links to VS Code for the Web. Supplied Apollo radio recordings accompany landing and footprints with explicit replay controls and global mute. Desktop testing is the current priority by user request.
