# Sol-3 exploration museum release — October 20, 2026

Changes since the October 7 production release (`00d4af3`, documented by `e06f111`). The authorized experience still covers Apollo 11, Sojourner, Spirit, Opportunity and Voyager 1; this release adds no later chapters.

## Added

- A five-exhibit museum entrance with shared 3D spacecraft, question-led NASA-linked stories, native scrolling and restored browsing position when returning from a mission.
- A playable Sojourner arrival: fictional Moon departure, spaceflight to Mars and manual landing at Ares Vallis. Assistance, braking, pause, retry and independent travel/landing skips are available.
- Piloted Mars surface journeys between Ares Vallis, Gusev Crater and Meridiani Planum. Courses include guide lights, steering, acceleration, braking, obstacles, two checkpoints, recovery and optional cruise assistance.
- Twenty optional Miso questions: five each for Sojourner, Spirit, Opportunity and Voyager. Each gives explanatory feedback and a NASA source. Immediate repeats are excluded, including when browser storage is unavailable.
- An original white EVA-style explorer, procedural Martian rock/weathering details, distinct Gusev hills and Meridiani ripples/outcrops.

## Changed from the previous version

- The museum is the starting point; choosing an exhibit launches its journey. All five missions remain freely accessible through **Explore other missions**.
- Opening an unknown object identifies it immediately. The separate scan button/timer is removed; meaningful evidence and assembly investigations still gate saved discoveries.
- Prominent archive terminology becomes **Notebook** and **Save discovery**.
- Mars soil and lighting now share Chapter 2's warm palette while preserving site-specific terrain and rock forms.
- Mars flight maps show the destination ahead at the top, named endpoints, checkpoints and a larger directional marker. Geographic coordinates retain their original east-positive, wrapped interpolation.
- Regional surface maps have readable landing-site labels. Evidence-inspection maps sit at the bottom-left with no expandable travel menu. Rover/Pathfinder hardware and MER mission-record inspections hide the map. Apollo and Voyager remain map-free.
- Exploration buttons have brighter borders, selected hardware outlines are subtler, and the Sojourner discovery strip fits its available clues.

## Engineering and verification

The React/TypeScript/Vite application retains one R3F canvas, renderer and camera. Museum and shuttle scenes use the retained-world architecture; Moon/Mars landing share a controller. Chapter transitions preserve discoveries and inspection exit restores the scene and focus.

Focused desktop runs cover the complete Apollo journey, all five hardware zoom/reset/exit flows, museum scroll restoration, forward/reverse Mars routes, saved discoveries, inspection clearance, checkpoint recovery and reduced motion. The latest map adjustment passed its focused inspection regression and visual review.

Validation results are recorded in [STATUS.md](STATUS.md). No single full-suite pass is claimed. Mobile testing was excluded at the user's request. The existing Three.js bundle-size advisory remains.

## Interpretation and provenance

Miso, shuttle flight, compressed routes, interactive geology and hardware separation are fictional or schematic teaching devices. Real mission facts link to official NASA sources. Terrain and object placements are not exact archaeological surveys, geographic navigation charts or rover telemetry.

No paid media generation or new external runtime service is introduced. Local video production, generated QA captures and build output are excluded from the source release.
